## A1 Multimeter basics

Owner's notes. Any cheap digital meter with DC volts, AC volts, ohms and a diode setting does everything on these pages. Thin, pointed probes matter more than the meter.

### Four settings, four jobs

| Setting | Use it for | Read it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DC volts, 200 V range | Every supply rail, coil and lamp voltage, the battery | A rail that is missing reads 0; a rail that is low by more than 10 % is a fault |
| AC volts | General illumination only (6.3 V AC). GI on the DC setting reads nonsense | About 6.3 V AC at the string when it is fully on |
| Ohms (Ω) | Coils, fuses, wires, lamp sockets. **Game off** | Under 1 Ω is a short, OL is open, anything else compare with a known-good twin |
| Diode | Coil diodes, switch diodes, bridge rectifiers, driver transistors. **Game off** | 0.4–0.7 V one way and OL the other is healthy; low both ways is shorted; OL both ways is open |

### The rails in this machine

The names printed on the boards are the historical ones. The real readings on a healthy WPC-89 driver board are in [A5 Power driver board](#find:A5). Solenoids run on the "50 V" rail, which actually reads about 70 V. Flashers run on the 20 V rail, the lamp matrix on 18 V, logic on 5 V and 12 V. Mains lives in the power box, at the transformer and in the service outlet under the cabinet: never probe there.

### Rules that save boards

1. **Power off for ohms and diode tests.** Voltage on the probes ruins the reading and can ruin the meter.
2. **Unplug what you measure.** A coil measured through its connector reads through every other coil on that connector. A driver transistor measured with the coils plugged in reads through the coil. Pull the connector first, then measure.
3. **One probe at a time into a connector.** Probe .156 Molex housings from the wire side with a thin probe, with the other probe on ground (TP5 on the driver board, or a bare chassis screw). Bridging two neighbouring pins with 70 V on one of them is how driver transistors die.
4. **Fuses come out to be tested.** In the holder a fuse can read fine through the circuit around it. Out, on ohms: under 1 Ω good, OL blown. Values are in the [Fuse List](#find:Fuse List).
5. **Do not trust the coin door.** The coin door parts list in this manual (page 2-30) includes an interlock switch, which cuts the 50 V and 20 V rails when the door opens. Check that yours is there and works, and even then measure before you touch.
6. **Cleaning is not repair.** A bad reading at a connector is measured and looked at before anything is sprayed. [A7 Connectors and cleaning](#find:A7) has the decision.
7. **A meter averages.** The switch and lamp matrices are pulsed. A meter on a matrix line shows a blurry average, not a signal. Use the game's own tests (T.1 Switch Edges, T.10 Lamp Test) or a logic probe for those.
8. **Zero the leads before low ohms.** Touch the probes together on the ohms range and read: that is the leads, and it comes off every coil reading. The owner's meter reads 0.1–0.2 Ω shorted, so a coil showing 4.9 Ω is 4.7–4.8 Ω. Below about 5 Ω that is the difference between a good coil and a suspicious one.

### Where things are

The driver board (A-12697) is on the left of the backbox; test points and the big fuses are there. The CPU board is on the right with the batteries. The Fliptronics board is at the top. The High Power board (A-15139) for the magnets and the Extra Flipper Supply board (A-15416) for the upper flippers are under the playfield. Every switch, lamp and solenoid page in this app names its connector pin, driver transistor and fuse, so read those first.
