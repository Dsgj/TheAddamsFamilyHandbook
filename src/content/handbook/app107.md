## A7 Connectors and cleaning

Owner's notes. Contact cleaner is not a repair tool. Most "clean the connector" advice on the internet skips the step that matters: looking at the connector and measuring it first. This page is the process the Care page's yearly round follows.

### Cleaning is not repair

Eight different things get called "a bad contact". Only the first two are fixed with a spray.

| What it is | What fixes it |
| --- | --- |
| Dirt, dust, oil, old spray residue | Solvent: IPA or an electronics cleaner, then a contact treatment |
| Light oxidation on intact pins | DeoxIT D5 or Kontakt 60 PLUS |
| Browned, melted or darkened housing or pins (heat) | Replace both sides: new terminals in a new housing and a new header on the board |
| Loose terminal, weak spring tension, terminal that backs out | Replace the terminal, with a proper crimp tool |
| Poor crimp or a tired IDC connection | Replace with a crimped Trifurcon terminal; never squeeze a crimp with pliers |
| Cracked solder joint at a header or a lug | Reflow or resolder |
| Failed component (diode, transistor, coil, bulb) | Diagnose with [A1 Multimeter basics](#find:A1) and the page for that part, then replace it |
| Battery leakage on the CPU board | Neutralise, clean, repair traces: [A5 Power driver board](#find:A5). A spray does nothing here |

### The decision for every connector

Power off. Pull the connector and look at both sides in good light.

1. **Clean and reuse** when it is mechanically intact, not heat-marked, not badly corroded, the terminals grip the pins, and it is merely dirty or dull. Clean if needed, treat with D5 or Kontakt 60 PLUS, push it back, then verify the circuit works.
2. **Clean, then a precise treatment** when the contact is accessible and already clean: a very small amount of DeoxIT D100L, wipe off the excess so only a thin film remains.
3. **Replace** when it is burnt, browned, melted, badly oxidised, the terminals are loose, the crimp or IDC is unreliable, the header pins are damaged, or the circuit still measures a voltage drop across the connector after cleaning. Replace the terminal and, when the pins are marked, the header too.

Do not replace a connector only because the machine is old. Do not clean a burnt connector and call the job done.

### Products, and what each one is for

| Product | Use | Do not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DeoxIT D5** (5 % DeoxIT in 75 % mineral spirits and 20 % propellant, per CAIG) | Intact connectors that are dirty or lightly oxidised: it flushes, deoxidises and leaves a thin protective film | Fog whole boards, switches, optos or plastics with it |
| **Kontakt Chemie 60 PLUS** | The European one-can equivalent of D5: its maker says it removes oxides and sulphides and leaves a protective film that reduces friction and wear | Confuse it with plain Kontakt 60 |
| **Plain Kontakt 60** | The aggressive first step of the three-product system Kontakt 60, then Kontakt WL, then Kontakt 61 | Use it alone; the maker's own instruction is to rinse with Kontakt WL and protect with Kontakt 61 |
| **DeoxIT D100L** (100 % active) | Precise application on contacts that are already clean, where overspray is unwanted | Use it as a cleaner; it does not flush |
| **High-purity IPA / residue-free electronics cleaner** | Dirt, oil, grease, flux, and anywhere no film is wanted | Expect it to deoxidise or protect; it only cleans |
| **WD-40, 5-56, oil, grease** | Nothing in this machine | Ever, on contacts, switches, plungers or sleeves |

One flushing product (D5 or 60 PLUS), one precise product (D100L) and IPA is the whole chemical kit. The rest of the kit is what actually repairs things: a multimeter, a proper crimp tool, phosphor-bronze Trifurcon terminals (.156 for the driver board connectors, .100 for the CPU board), a few connector housings and headers, card stock for switches, and a soldering iron fit for board work.

### Switches are not connectors

The leaf switches, the Fliptronics end-of-stroke switches and the optos are low-current gold contacts. They never see a spray. Close the contacts on clean card stock and pull it through; IPA on the card if something sticky is on them. No files, no abrasive paper, no burnishing. A pitted, misaligned or worn switch is adjusted or replaced, not cleaned. A switch that misbehaves gets a diagnosis first: gap, wire, solder at the lug, diode, connector, matrix, see [A3 Switches and optos](#find:A3).

### Symptoms are not causes

An intermittent switch, coil, lamp or motor is a reason to measure, not to spray. Before any chemical: connector continuity, terminal tension, solder joints, wire continuity, switch adjustment, switch diode, driver transistor, coil diode and tieback path, mechanical binding, the header pins on the board. The appendix page for that kind of part has the measurements.

### The yearly round, in priority order

The order is by how expensive the failure is if missed, not by how often it happens. The Care page tracks each one.

1. **CPU board battery area**: leakage spreads and eats traces. [A5 Power driver board](#find:A5).
2. **J122 and both diode tieback groups**: a lost tieback destroys a driver transistor and can leave a motor or coil locked on. [A2 Coils, magnets and motors](#find:A2).
3. **GI connectors J115, J120, J121**: the amps run through them and they brown. [A4 Lamps, flashers and GI](#find:A4).
4. **Other high-current connectors**: the solenoid connectors J122–J130, the flipper connectors, the power input, headers and solder joints.
5. **A look over every board**: cracked solder, overheated components, corrosion, damaged traces, earlier repairs, tired connectors.
6. **Switches**: clean and adjust only the ones that fail T.1 or feel wrong.
7. **Everything else**: clean and treat where the inspection justified it.

After any work on a tieback, a burnt connector or a battery repair, verify electrically before the glass goes back on: the solenoid test for the loads, the test points for the rails, T.1 for the switches.
