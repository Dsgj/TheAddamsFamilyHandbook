## A4 Lamps, flashers and GI

Owner's notes. Three different circuits light this machine and they fail differently. The lamp page in this app gives the matrix column and row with their transistor and pin; the solenoid page covers flashers; the GI table on the Solenoids page gives the five strings.

### The lamp matrix, 18 V DC

Eight columns from J137 through Q98–Q91, eight rows from J133 through Q90–Q83, driven in turn 8 times a cycle. The bulbs are #555 wedge in the inserts and jets (part 24-8768).

| What T.10 Lamp Test shows | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| One lamp out | Bulb, then socket. Swap in a known-good bulb; then ohms across the socket contacts with the bulb in, game off |
| One lamp dim or flickers | Socket contacts tarnished or the wedge base loose; bend the socket contacts in slightly |
| A whole column out | The column transistor on the lamp page (Q9x) or its J137 pin |
| A whole row out | The row transistor (Q8x) or its J133 pin |
| A column or row stuck fully on | That transistor shorted |
| Odd lamps glow faintly | Ghosting, see LEDs below |

**With the meter**, game off, diode setting on the transistor as in [A2 Coils, magnets and motors](#find:A2). With the game on and the test on "all lamps", DC volts at a socket shows a pulsed average, a few volts; it is the comparison between a working socket and a dead one that tells you something, not the number. For a dead column or row the clean test is a known-good #555 pushed straight into the column pin and row pin at the board with the harness unplugged: if it lights, the board is fine and the fault is in the harness or sockets.

### LEDs and ghosting

The LED kit replaces the #555 and #44 bulbs. Two things follow:

- **Ghosting**: WPC-89 lamp timing leaks a little current into the next column, invisible on a filament, a faint glow on an LED. Non-ghosting LEDs hide it, and there is a ghost-busting patch for the TAF ROM that fixes the timing. If lamps glow that should be off after the kit, that is the cause, not a fault.
- **GI flicker when dimmed**: the GI is dimmed by triacs chopping the AC. LEDs show that as flicker. Setup step 3 sets A.1 25 Allow Dim Illumination to NO for that reason.

### Flashers, 20 V DC

The #906 wedge flashers (17–22) are driven as solenoids from the 20 V rail through the transistor on their solenoid page, fuse F111 for the secondary group. Test them in the solenoid test, and if one is dead: bulb, socket, then the transistor exactly as a coil. A #906 and a #555 have the same base shape and are not interchangeable.

### General illumination, 6.3 V AC

Five strings, all through J120 and J121 on the driver board, each with its own triac and fuse. The two playfield strings use #44 bayonet bulbs, the two insert strings #555.

| String | Pin | Triac | Fuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GI 1 Left Playfield | J120-1 | Q18 | F110 |
| GI 2 Insert House | J120-2 | Q10 | F106 |
| GI 3 Insert People | J120-3 | Q14 | F107 |
| GI 4 not used | J121-5 | Q16 | F109 |
| GI 5 Right Playfield | J121-6 | Q12 | F108 |

- **A whole string out**: the fuse first, out of the holder. Then AC volts at the pin on J120/J121: about 6.3 V AC with the string on. Volts at the pin and no light means the harness or a bad socket; no volts means the triac or a burnt connector.
- **The connectors**: J115 (the 6.3 V AC feed from the transformer) and J120/J121 (the strings out) carry amps and brown with age. Browned, loose or melted pins are the single most common GI fault on WPC. Look, do not assume: discoloured housing or pins, weak terminal tension, a poor crimp, cracked solder at the header, heat marks on the board around it. If there is heat damage, replace both sides, terminals and header. If it is clean and tight, leave it alone. The decision tree is in [A7 Connectors and cleaning](#find:A7).
- **Half a string out**: a broken wire or a socket where the daisy-chain passes through; GI sockets are in series along the string's wire, so the break is between the last lit and the first dark bulb.
