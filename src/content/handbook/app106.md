## A6 Flippers

Owner's notes. TAF is a Fliptronics I game: the flippers are driven by the Fliptronics board at the top of the backbox, not by the switch matrix and not by the EOS switch directly. Four flippers, four coil types are not all the same.

### The coils

Each flipper coil has two windings sharing one lug: a low-ohm power winding for the stroke and a high-ohm hold winding for keeping the flipper up. Measure from the common lug to each of the other two, game off, connector unplugged.

| Flipper | Coil | Power winding | Hold winding | Power from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lower left, lower right | FL-15411 orange | 4.2 Ω | 145 Ω | Driver board, F101 / F102 |
| Upper right | FL-11630 red | 4.7 Ω | 160 Ω | Extra Flipper Supply board, F902 |
| Upper left (Thing Flips) | FL-11753 yellow | 9.8 Ω | 165 Ω | Extra Flipper Supply board, F901 |

- Power winding OL: no stroke at all, the flipper only creeps up on the hold winding.
- Hold winding OL: a strong stroke that drops straight back down.
- Either winding under 1 Ω or well below the table: shorted, fuse blows, replace the coil and test the transistor.
- The upper flippers and the three Power magnets get their 50 V (really 70 V) from the Extra Flipper Supply board A-15416, in the backbox just to the right of and above the driver board, photo below. If both upper flippers or all the magnets are dead, measure there first: J903 pins 1 and 2 carry the 50 V to the magnet board under the playfield, pins 3 and 4 are its ground. The manual's schematic for this board is wrong on both counts: J903 is drawn incorrectly and the bridge rectifier is rotated 90 degrees. Fuses F901 and F902 are on this board.

### What the EOS does on this game

The end-of-stroke switch on a Fliptronics game is a **normally open** switch that closes when the flipper reaches the top. It carries no coil current. The board reads it (F1, F3, F5, F7 on the J806 table) and switches the coil from the power winding to the hold winding when it closes. If the ball knocks the flipper down, the EOS opens and the board gives the power winding another pulse.

- **EOS never closes** (dirty, gap too wide): the flipper still works, because the software falls back to a timer, but it drops under a hard ball hit and hold is weak.
- **EOS stuck closed** (bent, gap gone): the board thinks the flipper is already up, so it gives hold only: a lazy or dead flipper.
- Gap 0.062 inch (1.6 mm) at rest, closing just before the end of travel. Clean with a card, never file. The manual disagrees with itself on the EOS type: the parts list gives SW-1A-194 (make, normally open) for the lower flippers and SW-1A-193 for the upper ones, while the flipper assembly note on page 2-16 describes contacts that open at end of stroke. Look at yours at rest: contacts apart means normally open. The Verify page tracks it.

### Weak or lazy flipper, in this order

1. **Rubber**: hard or flat rubber shoots wide and dead.
2. **Sleeve**: pull the plunger; a grey, gritty or scored sleeve is replaced, never lubricated. Some replacement sleeves are 1/8 inch short and let the plunger slop.
3. **Plunger and link**: a mushroomed plunger tip or a worn link (the first parts round replaces these, A-10656 × 4).
4. **Coil stop**: mushroomed or loose stop changes the stroke.
5. **Bushing**: a worn flipper bushing lets the bat sit low and drag on the playfield.
6. **EOS**: as above.
7. **Coil**: the table above.
8. **Transistor**: the Fliptronics board uses a TIP102 driving a TIP36C per winding. Test as in [A2 Coils, magnets and motors](#find:A2), with the flipper connector unplugged.
9. **Connector pins**: browned pins at the Fliptronics board or at the coil.

### The buttons

The cabinet buttons on this game are leaf switches, not optos: the button assembly B-12273-6 in the cabinet parts list, with a stacked pair of blade switches behind each button. On the right side the lower pair is F2 (blue-violet, J805-1) and the upper pair F6 (black-yellow, J805-3); on the left F4 (blue-gray, J805-2) and F8 (black-blue, J805-5). All four share one switch ground, the orange wire on J805-6, so each button's connector carries three wires: two signals and the ground. A button that works only when pressed hard is a blade gap or dirty contacts, cleaned with card stock like any switch, see [A3 Switches and optos](#find:A3). Both flippers dead on one side and the other side fine is nearly always that button's switch stack or its three-pin connector, not the coils. Opto flipper boards with a metal flag came on later WPC games; if one has been retrofitted here it needs 12 V and ground as well.

### Tuning, in the order the wear happens

1. **The link first.** With the playfield up, push the flipper link by hand and watch for play before the bat moves. A worn link is most of what a tune-up needs; the first parts round replaces all four (A-10656).
2. **Level.** Sluggish flippers on a game that is off level are the level, not the flippers.
3. **Removing a flipper**, when the sleeve, plunger, stop or bushing has to come out: loosen the bat clamp with a 3/8-inch wrench (a ratcheting one makes the clamp easy; an Allen key holds the shaft if it turns) and pull the bat out from the top. Take the socket-head screws out of the rear of the coil, lift off the stop, slide the coil off the mechanism. Two screws free the EOS switch. Eight screws hold the flipper plate to the playfield.
4. **A coil that will not slide off** has distorted from heat and is replaced, not persuaded.
5. **The bushing**: three screws into the plate with locknuts on the threads. Replace it if the bat sits low or rocks.
6. **New plungers**: some replacement plungers have had loose roll pins that fall out after a few hundred games. Check the pin is tight before it goes in; a dab of thread-locking glue on it is cheap insurance.
7. Then coil stop, plunger, link and clamp as needed, and the EOS if it looks tired.

### The Fliptronics I board, and its successor

The Addams Family and Addams Family Gold are the only games with the Fliptronics I board (first photo below). It is repairable, and a Fliptronics II board (any of A-15472, A-15072-1, A-15472-1; second photo) drops onto the same mounting holes if it ever has to be replaced. The connector mapping, originally from M. McAndrew via Clay Harrel's WPC guide:

| Fliptronics II | Fliptronics I |
| --- | --- |
| J902 | J802 |
| J905 | J805 |
| J906 | J806 |
| J904 | J804 |
| J903 | J803 (ribbon cable) |
| J901, J907 | not used |

On the driver board, remove J111 and leave J109, J110 and J112 connected. On the Extra Flipper Supply board, J901, J902 and J903 stay as they are.

### Never

No oil, grease or spray on plungers, sleeves, links or the EOS. A flipper that squeaks needs a new sleeve.
