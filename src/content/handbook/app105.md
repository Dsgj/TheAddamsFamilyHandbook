## A5 Power driver board

Owner's notes on the WPC-89 power driver board, A-12697, left side of the backbox. Almost every "the game does something strange" fault starts with five minutes on its test points.

### Test points, game on, black probe on TP5

| Test point | Printed | Healthy reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TP2 | 5 V | 4.9–5.1 V DC, and it must not dip when the flippers fire |
| TP3 | 12 V | 12.0 V DC regulated |
| TP1 | 12 V | 14–15 V DC, unregulated, feeds the regulator |
| TP8 | 18 V | 17–19 V DC, lamp matrix |
| TP7 | 20 V | 22–25 V DC, flashers and the magnets' group |
| TP6 | 50 V | 70–75 V DC, solenoids and flippers. Yes, 70 |

A rail at zero: its fuse, then the bridge rectifier that makes it. A rail that is low: the bridge or the big filter capacitor next to it. The 5 V rail is the one that matters most, see resets.

### Resets

The CPU board's watchdog resets the game the moment 5 V dips under about 4.65 V, even for a few milliseconds, typically when both flippers fire during multiball. The chain that makes 5 V is: transformer, bridge BR2, filter capacitor C5 (18 000 µF), the LM323K regulator on its heat sink, then the connectors to the CPU board.

1. Watch TP2 while someone plays. If it sags with the flippers, keep going down this list.
2. **Bridge BR2**, game off, diode setting on each of its four legs: 0.5–0.6 V forward, OL reverse. A bridge that has lost a tenth of a volt with age can pass this test and still be the cause; if everything else checks out, replace it.
3. **C5**: it is usually blamed and often still fine. Replace it as one step, not as the fix.
4. **The LM323K** and its heat sink: if the regulator runs very hot, the board is starving downstream. A drop-in switching regulator replacement runs cool and is a common upgrade.
5. **Connectors** on the 5 V path to the CPU board: reseat, look for browned pins.

### Bridge rectifiers in general

Four legs, two marked ~ (AC in), one + and one −. Diode setting, game off, ideally with the board's connectors off: from − to each ~ and from each ~ to + should read 0.5–0.6 V; the other direction OL. One leg shorted takes its fuse out at power-up; one leg open gives a rail at roughly half the voltage with a lot of hum in the lamps.

### The batteries

Three AA cells in series on the CPU board, 4.5 V fresh. Measure across the pack with the game off; at the RAM (U8, pins 26–28) about 4.3 V is expected. Below about 4 V the settings will not survive a power cycle and the game boots with "Factory Settings Restored". Any white or green crust on the holder is a leak, and it is the first thing to check on the yearly round, because it keeps spreading under the solder mask. Contact cleaner does nothing for it.

1. Look at the holder, the board around it, the component leads nearby and the copper: white or green crust, dark copper, eaten leads.
2. Assume it goes further than you can see; look under the holder and at the traces and vias on both sides of the board.
3. Neutralise the alkaline residue (household white vinegar on a swab), rinse with IPA, dry completely.
4. Repair eaten traces, vias, sockets and component leads; the comparator and the reset circuit next to the holder are the usual victims.
5. If the original holder is still on the board, consider a remote holder on a lead or an NVRAM module so a future leak lands nowhere near the board.

Setup step 7 records the change date.

### A fuse that keeps blowing

Never fit a bigger fuse. Find the load with the [Fuse List](#find:Fuse List) and the fuse column on the solenoid pages, then:

1. Unplug the connector for everything on that fuse. Power up. If the fuse holds, the fault is downstream, not on the board.
2. Each coil on that fuse, on ohms, against its twin: [A2 Coils, magnets and motors](#find:A2).
3. Each coil diode, diode setting.
4. The driver transistors for the group, connector still unplugged.
5. For a lamp fuse, a shorted bulb or socket pulling the 18 V rail down; for a GI fuse, the triac and the J120/J121 pins: [A4 Lamps, flashers and GI](#find:A4).
6. If the fuse blows with everything unplugged, the bridge for that rail is shorted.
