## A2 Coils, magnets and motors

Owner's notes. Every solenoid page in this app gives the coil type, the driver transistor (Q number), the connector pin and the fuse. This page says what to expect when you put a meter on them.

### Coil types in this machine

Measured across the two lugs, game off, connector unplugged, at room temperature. Values are typical; allow ±10 %. The best reference is a twin in the same machine: the five jet bumpers are all AE-26-1200, so any one of them tells you what the others should read.

### Reading a coil

- **Under 1 Ω**: shorted winding. The coil locks on or blows its fuse the first time it fires. Replace the coil, then test the driver transistor below, because a shorted coil often takes it with it.
- **OL**: open winding, a broken wire at the lug or a cracked solder joint. Wiggle the wires at the lugs while measuring.
- **Right value but weak**: the coil is fine. Look at the sleeve, plunger and mechanism, then at the transistor and the connector pins.

### The coil diode

Every coil has a diode across its lugs to absorb the kick when the coil switches off. Diode setting, game off: black probe on the banded end reads 0.4–0.7 V, swapped probes read OL. Low both ways is a shorted diode; it looks like a shorted coil on the ohms setting, so lift one leg of the diode and measure the coil alone if the reading is ambiguous. A shorted diode puts the whole rail across the driver transistor when it fires: transistor and fuse go. A missing or open diode lets the kick back into the driver board: the transistor dies a little later instead.

### The driver transistor

The Q number on the solenoid page is a TIP102 for the low-power and flasher circuits and a TIP102 driving a TIP36C for the high-power circuits. Unplug the coil connector for that group first. Diode setting between the transistor's metal tab (collector) and its emitter leg: a reading near 0 both ways is a shorted transistor. With the game on and the connector unplugged, DC volts on the collector pin at the connector: it should sit at the rail voltage and drop when the solenoid test fires that coil. A coil that pulls in the moment the game powers up, without being told, is a shorted transistor. The pre-driver transistor next to it can fail the same way.

### Which one is it, in order

1. The fuse for the group, out of its holder, on ohms.
2. Every coil on that fuse, unplugged, on ohms, compared with its twin.
3. Every diode on those coils, diode setting.
4. The driver transistors for the group, coils still unplugged.
5. The connector pins: browned, loose or backed-out pins at J122–J130 mimic a bad coil.

### This machine's own traps

- **J122 carries two shared diode tieback groups**, not one. The four loads on J122 (25 Thing Motor Q26, 26 Thing Eject Hole Q24, 27 Bookcase Motor Q22, 28 Swamp Release Q20, all on fuse F103) have their flyback diodes tied back to the board in pairs:

  | Tieback wire | J122 pins | Shared by |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Gray-yellow | 5 and 8 | 25 Thing Motor (Q26) and 27 Bookcase Motor (Q22) |
  | Violet-green | 6 and 9 | 26 Thing Eject Hole (Q24) and 28 Swamp Release (Q20) |

  If a tieback wire, its IDC terminal, its solder joint at the load or the diode is open, the driver transistor takes the full flyback kick and dies; a dead transistor can leave that motor or coil locked on and cooking. The violet-green Swamp Release side is the well-known TAF failure, but inspect both groups after any work under the playfield. **Continuity test**, game off, J122 unplugged: from the J122 pin to the supply-side lug at the load along the tieback wire itself reads close to 0 Ω. A path that runs through a coil or motor winding reads several ohms, and that is the winding, not a bad tieback. Look at the J122 housing, the IDC terminals, the header pins and the board solder for browning, looseness or cracks; a doubtful IDC terminal is replaced with a crimped Trifurcon terminal, [A7 Connectors and cleaning](#find:A7). After any repair here, run the solenoid test on all four loads before the glass goes back. The photo below shows what a lost tieback does to the transistor.
- **The three Power magnets** run through the High Power board A-15139 under the playfield: Q44 for the left magnet on J127-9 (fuse F104), Q34 and Q32 for the upper and right magnets on J126-7 and J126-8 (fuse F111). The 20-9247 coil has a thermistor in it; a magnet that works when cold and fades after a long game is that thermistor doing its job, not a fault. The manual's solenoid wiring diagram (page 3-9 of the schematic manual) draws the High Power board on the J107 side; it is really fed from J126 (upper and right magnets) and J127 (left magnet), and its 50 V comes from the Extra Flipper Supply board's J903, see [A6 Flippers](#find:A6). A magnet transistor that shorts on leaves the magnet energised and burns the playfield under it, so the fuses on F104 and F111 must be the listed values, never larger.
- **The Thing Magnet** (06) is a different coil, A-12158-1 on J130-7 through Q66, fuse F105.
- **The motors** (25 Thing Motor, 27 Bookcase Motor) are on the flasher group with fuse F103. Test them in the solenoid test and listen; a motor that hums without turning has a jammed gear train, not an electrical fault.
