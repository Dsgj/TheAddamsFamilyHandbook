## A3 Switches and optos

Owner's notes. The switch page in this app gives the column and row, the wire colours, the CPU board pins (J206 for columns, J208 for rows) and the IC pin (U20 for columns, U18 or U19 for rows).

### How the matrix works, in one paragraph

The CPU strobes the eight columns one at a time through U20, a ULN2803 driver. The CPU board parts list (A-12742-2001) shows sockets only for the 40-pin CPU, the 32-pin game ROM and the 84-pin ASIC, so on an original board U20 is soldered in; fit a socket when it is replaced. Each row sits high, about 12 V through a pull-up, and is read by an LM339 comparator at U18 or U19. When a switch closes, its column strobe reaches its row through the switch and the small diode on the switch, and the row goes low for that column's moment. The diode is what keeps a closed switch from back-feeding other columns; that is why a bad diode shows up as other switches, not as this one.

### Symptom first

| What T.1 Switch Edges shows | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| One switch dead | The switch itself: gap, dirt, the diode open, a broken wire at the lug |
| One switch stuck closed | Bent blade, ball resting on it, or a shorted diode on **that** switch |
| A whole column or a whole row reads closed, or several switches fire when one is pressed | A shorted diode somewhere on that column or row, a pinched harness, or U20 / the row comparator |
| A whole column dead | U20 (the column driver) or J206 pin |
| A whole row dead | U18/U19 input or J208 pin |
| Switches fire at random after work under the playfield | A wire pulled off a lug or a diode leg broken |

### With the meter, game off

- **The switch**: continuity across the blades, pressed and released. A leaf switch wants a 1/16-inch (1.6 mm) gap. Clean the contacts by closing them on a clean business card and pulling it through; never file them.
- **The diode**: diode setting across it. 0.4–0.7 V one way, OL the other. Low both ways is shorted; OL both ways is open. Direction and banding as drawn on the switch pages.
- **Finding the shorted diode on a column**: unplug J206 and J208 so the board is out of it, then measure each diode on that column one by one. PinWiki describes a quicker adapter trick that swaps the column and row cables so the switch test shows only the bad one.
- **The wire**: continuity from the switch lug to the pin at J206 or J208. The pin numbers are on the switch page.

### With the game on

Rows and columns are pulsed, so a meter gives an average, not a level. Use T.1 with a ball for rollovers, a finger for targets and a card through the optos. If you own a logic probe, columns should pulse and rows should pulse only while a switch in them is closed.

### Optos

TAF has a 7-Opto board (A-14977) and a 4-Opto board (A-15017). Each opto is an infrared LED facing a phototransistor.

- The LED side runs from 12 V through a series resistor. The manual's parts list for the 2-opto switch assembly (page 2-13) reads 470 Ω, 1/2 W, as far as the scan is legible; the 7-opto board's values are not readable in the scan, so read the resistor on the board. The resistor runs warm; that is normal.
- **Phone test**: point a phone camera at the emitter with the game on. Most front cameras show infrared as a faint purple glow, so you can see at once whether the emitter is lit. A rear camera often has an infrared filter and shows nothing.
- **Receiver test**: DC volts on the receiver's output at the opto board with the beam open and with a card in it. It must swing between near 0 and near 12 V. No swing with a lit emitter is a dead phototransistor or the LM339 on the opto board.
- Check the 12 V at the opto board connector before condemning anything.

### Fliptronics switches

F1–F8 (the four end-of-stroke switches and the four flipper buttons) do not go through U20. They are read by the Fliptronics board, on the J806 table. The buttons are opto boards in the cabinet sides with a metal flag on the button. See [A6 Flippers](#find:A6).

### This machine's own traps

- **Center ramp shots award Millions Plus instead of Bear Kicks.** Both ramps award something on exit, and the game tells the two apart by which switch closed just before the shared exit switch. The switch near the top-left of the center ramp (the manual calls the center ramp the Right Ramp, so that is 65 Right Ramp Top; the shared one is 58 Right Ramp Exit) must close first. On one game the microswitch was worn out and was replaced; on another a small bend to the actuator made the shot register. Confirm in T.1 which switches a slow ball on the ramp actually closes before you bend anything.

### What kills U20

A solenoid, flasher, GI or lamp wire touching a switch wire puts 18–70 V into a column. U20 dies first and usually alone; the LM339 row comparators often survive. Replace U20, then find the wire that touched, or it happens again. If an LM339 must go, socket the replacement: the pads lift easily.
